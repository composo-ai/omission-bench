You are a meticulous clinical auditor working on an AI-evaluation research study. A colleague read
the TRANSCRIPT below and extracted a candidate list of the facts a correct clinical note of this
consultation must contain. Your job is to AUDIT that list against the transcript and grade what
survives. You are auditing the fact list, not any note.

TRANSCRIPT:
{transcript}

CANDIDATE FACTS:
{facts_block}

Do three things.

1. DROP a candidate fact if any of these hold:
   - NOT SUPPORTED: the transcript does not establish it, or establishes only a weaker or hedged
     version of it, or the supporting turn is garbled, ambiguous or contradicted elsewhere.
   - DUPLICATE: another surviving item already asserts the same thing (keep the clearer one, drop
     the other; two genuinely different elements of one topic are not duplicates).
   - NOT NOTE-BEARING: conversational colour, rapport, closure, identity checks or generic
     patient education that a clinical note has no duty to carry.

2. KEEP every fact that survives, with its ORIGINAL id. You may tighten the wording for
   precision, but never change what it asserts, and never merge two ids into one.

3. GRADE each surviving fact for severity, per the study's rubric. The question graded is: if the
   note carried this error - here, if the note OMITTED this fact - how much would it matter for
   the patient or the next clinician who reads the note? Grade the consequence of the error in
   the note, not the drama of the topic.

   "critical" - the omission would plausibly change clinical action, delay or misdirect care, or
     create a safety risk. Anchors: an omitted working diagnosis, or a hedged impression that
     would harden into certainty; omitted red-flag safety-netting ("return if the headache
     becomes sudden"); an omitted or wrong drug, allergy, dose, frequency, duration or
     interaction; a conditional plan made definite; an omitted examination that did happen, or a
     fabricated one; an omitted pertinent negative that licenses the management decision (the
     denied cardiac features behind treating chest pain as musculoskeletal); patient identity
     attributes used clinically.
   "supporting" - degrades the note's completeness, clarity or defensibility, but is unlikely to
     change what happens next. Anchors: duration or onset detail that colours but does not gate
     the plan; social context relevant to follow-up with no immediate action attached; nuance
     lost without meaning being inverted; a secondary symptom that neither supports nor threatens
     the diagnosis.
   "peripheral" - no plausible clinical consequence. Anchors: conversational colour, rapport,
     patient-education phrasing, administrative closure, redundant restatement of something
     already captured elsewhere.

   Decide in order: (1) ACTION test - would a competent GP, or the next clinician reading the
   note, plausibly DO something different if this fact were missing? yes -> critical. (2) SAFETY
   test - even if action today is unchanged, does its absence remove a safety net or create a
   latent risk? yes -> critical. (3) RECORD-QUALITY test - does its absence materially weaken the
   note as a clinical record? yes -> supporting. (4) Otherwise peripheral. If genuinely torn
   between two grades after the procedure, take the LOWER grade. Grade each fact independently;
   never curve to a target distribution.

Output ONLY a single valid JSON object:
{"facts": [{"id": "f1", "fact": "...", "severity": "critical", "why": "<=12 words"}],
 "dropped": [{"id": "f7", "reason": "unsupported" | "duplicate" | "not-note-bearing"}]}
Every id in the candidate list must appear exactly once, in "facts" or in "dropped". Escape all
newlines inside strings as \n. No commentary, no markdown fences.
